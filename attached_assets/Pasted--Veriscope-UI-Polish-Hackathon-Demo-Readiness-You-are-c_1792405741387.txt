## Primary goal

Improve the visual design and user experience without compromising working functionality.

The product should look like a credible enterprise risk management platform, not a generic AI dashboard or a static prototype.

## 1. Design direction

Use a restrained, professional enterprise design:

- Navy and white as the primary palette.
- Teal as a restrained accent for meaningful highlights.
- Clear typography, spacing, alignment, and visual hierarchy.
- Consistent border radii, shadows, buttons, badges, and form controls.
- Accessible contrast and readable text.
- Responsive layouts for desktop and smaller screens.
- Avoid excessive gradients, neon colors, unnecessary animations, oversized cards, and decorative AI-themed graphics.

Inspect the existing design system and reuse its patterns where practical. Do not introduce a second, inconsistent design system.

## 2. Prioritize the main demo workflow

Inspect the existing routes, components, and screenshots before making changes. Then polish these areas in order:

### A. Main dashboard
- Make the overall risk situation immediately understandable.
- Present existing risk metrics clearly and consistently with actual backend data.
- Improve spacing, hierarchy, and the visual distinction between high-priority items and ordinary information.
- Do not invent statistics or add decorative metrics that do not come from real data.

### B. Risk register
- Improve table readability, row spacing, column alignment, and responsive behavior.
- Make risk severity, status, ownership, and review-needed indicators easy to scan.
- Keep filters, sorting, navigation, and other existing behavior working.

### C. Risk detail and evidence
- Make the risk description, severity, status, source excerpts, verification results, and review warnings easy to understand.
- Clearly distinguish verified evidence from partial, unsupported, or review-required findings.
- Ensure source excerpts are readable and their source document/page context is visible when available.
- Improve the assessment history so users can understand how a risk evolved over time.

### D. Reassessment and closure workflow
- Make the workflow intuitive: add evidence, reassess, review the result, and request human approval to close.
- Keep the distinction between an AI recommendation and an approved human decision explicit.
- Preserve the existing closing requirements and validation.
- Do not bypass or weaken guardrails to make the interface appear simpler.
- Ensure the current risk #5 demo data still displays correctly and references reassessment #10.

### E. Upload and feedback states
- Polish upload controls, supported-format information, loading indicators, success messages, validation errors, empty states, and retry guidance.
- Ensure errors remain understandable and do not imply that a failed analysis succeeded.
- Avoid unnecessary UI blocking or confusing duplicate actions.
